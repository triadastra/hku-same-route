COMP1110 group project · Modeling coincidences: accidentally having the same route · HKU, November 2026

Is that stranger following you, or just taking the quickest way?

We simulated one weekday for 10,000 people on the real footpaths from Kennedy Town to Sai Ying Pun and recorded every turn they made. Nobody in the simulation follows anyone. Even so, on 74% of trips a stranger made the same five turns in a row as the walker, each within 90 seconds of them. Long matching streaks like this come mostly from everyone choosing the same quickest routes.

Key findings

74%of trips had a stranger make the same 5 turns in a row as the walker, each within 90 seconds.
48%of trips still had one for 10 turns in a row.
13×more strangers share 10 turns with you when everyone takes the quickest path than when turns are random: 11.1 against 0.9 per trip.
49%of local residents' trips had a 5-turn match, against 73–81% for students and staff, who share the class-change crowds.

Results

A stranger shares a turn with you when you both pass the same junction, coming in the same way and leaving the same way, each within 90 seconds of the other. For each trip we find the stranger with the longest unbroken run of shared turns. The baseline re-walks the very same 57,000 trips (same start, end and departure time) with near-random turns.

Method

The map

Every footpath, staircase, lift and road from Kennedy Town to Sai Ying Pun, from OpenStreetMap: 3,543 junctions and 4,835 path segments, 156 km in all. Stairs walk 2.2 times slower, and a lift costs 40 seconds.

All 1,538 named buildings and amenities are places with a short caption, so a person can search "noodles", "toilet" or "study".

The people

10,000 synthetic people: 62% undergrads, 12% postgrads, 10% staff and 16% local residents. Each has a home (a hall, a flat, or the MTR exit or bus stop they commute through), fixed commitments such as classes in HKU's :30 to :20 slots, and drawn traits: personality, habits, interests, mood, how spontaneous they are and walking pace.

Their decisions

Each day was lived by Claude Haiku 5.5, acting through map tools like someone with a phone map: where_am_i, search_places, nearby, route_info, go_to, stroll, stay and end_day. Nobody was told what to do with free time. Variety comes from each person's traits; 22% chose to wander somewhere without a set route.

The measure

Walks follow the quickest path and record every segment entered, with the time, and every turn at a junction: left, right, straight or back. For each trip we find the longest run of turns a single stranger shared, each within 90 seconds. The random-turn baseline re-walks the same trips.

Limits

  • Synthetic people. The mix of roles, homes, timetables and traits is our estimate, not HKU data. The headline numbers depend on how many people share each class change.
  • The baseline is not a clean control. Random walkers take longer routes and pass more people, so they collect more short matches. Compare long runs (Figure 2), not short ones.
  • Nobody reacts to anyone. Deliberate following, crowds slowing people down and choosing a route to avoid someone are not modelled.
  • Map gaps. Indoor routes through buildings are mostly missing from OpenStreetMap, lighting is known for only 87 segments, and opening hours were not enforced.
  • One weekday. One run, with fine weather and normal classes. Weekends, exams and rain would change who walks where.
